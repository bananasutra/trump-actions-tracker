# 🙊 U.S. Democracy Gone Bananas: Trump Actions Tracker

**A real-time strategic diagnostic of systemic democratic erosion and institutional dismantling in the United States since January 2025.**

### 🔍 Project Overview
The **Trump Action Tracker** is a friendly data dashboard designed to monitor the velocity and impact of executive actions on the foundations of American democracy. This tool categorizes policy shifts into ten critical themes, including **Violating Democratic Norms**, **Hollowing the State**, **Suppressing Dissent**, and **Weaponizing Federal Institutions**.

### 📊 Key Strategic Insights
* **Institutional Rewrite:** Tracking the transition from policy disruption to a full administrative overhaul of the federal government.
* **Strategic Velocity:** Analyzing the "procedural shock" caused by high-volume executive orders designed to outpace judicial processing latency.
* **Norm-Collapse Loops:** Identifying "multi-tagged" actions that simultaneously weaken multiple institutional checks and balances.

### 🛠️ Methodology & Data

**Source.** This dashboard visualizes the **Trump Action Tracker** dataset, created and maintained by **Prof. Christina Pagel** — [trumpactiontracker.info](https://www.trumpactiontracker.info). Snapshots of her published CSV exports are loaded here; all collection, description and classification of actions is hers.

**What the ten domains mean.** The domain names used throughout this dashboard are defined in her [glossary](https://www.trumpactiontracker.info/glossary), which sets out the criteria for each and sources the framework to Levitsky & Ziblatt (*How Democracies Die*), Timothy Snyder (*The Road to Unfreedom*) and Anne Applebaum (*Twilight of Democracy*). Read it before interpreting any chart here — a tag means what the glossary says it means.

**Scope.** Per her [about page](https://www.trumpactiontracker.info/about), the dataset "records publicly reported actions, statements, policies, legal developments and plans." It is not limited to completed acts, and nothing in the data distinguishes an act from a plan or a statement — so counts here include all of these.

**Classifications are expressions of opinion.** This is her characterization, and it carries through to everything computed from the data. From the glossary, verbatim:

> "There is always some element of subjectivity in assigning actions to different domains... These classifications are expressions of opinion based on the cited sources and should not be read as findings of criminality, legal liability, or unlawful conduct unless expressly supported by a court judgment or official finding."

**Licensing.** See issue #1 — the repository currently states this inconsistently and the correct statement depends on a decision by this repo's maintainer. Her published terms are on the site and in line 1 of each CSV.
